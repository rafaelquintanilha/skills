# Editorial standard

This reference defines an opinionated standard for written Brazilian
Portuguese. It favors composed, precise prose with classical connective tissue,
controlled longer periods, and explicit reasoning. It does not attempt to
represent every legitimate Portuguese style.

Apply this house style to new drafts and explicitly requested stylistic
rewrites. When reviewing competent existing prose written in another
legitimate style, preserve that style and intervene only where the text has an
actual defect or the user requested reconsideration.

This distinction follows Almeida, §§980-981: grammar governs correctness,
whereas style remains the writer's individual manner of expression. The
opinionated standard may shape a new draft, but grammatical review must not
mistake uniformity for correctness.

## Compose in Portuguese

Start from meaning, not from the source sentence. Identify the propositions,
their hierarchy, and the relationships among them; then construct the sentence
in the order that reads naturally in Portuguese.

Prefer:

- a clear main clause, with subordinate clauses serving an identifiable
  purpose;
- direct modal verbs and explicit antecedents rather than circumlocution,
  nominal paraphrases, or vague references;
- verbal clauses over chains of abstract nouns when both express the same
  thought; composed prose should not become bureaucratic prose;
- explicit causal, concessive, conditional, temporal, or conclusive
  relationships where the reasoning depends on them;
- precise nouns and verbs over vague adjectives and intensifiers;
- enough context for the reader to understand the mechanism, consequence, or
  decision;
- paragraphs that develop a unit of thought instead of collecting sentences
  with similar rhythm;
- varied sentence length, including longer periods whose clauses remain easy to
  follow;
- ordinary Brazilian word order rather than literary inversion, enclisis, or
  mesoclisis introduced merely to make the prose appear formal;
- established connective expressions when they clarify the argument, used
  sparingly and according to meaning.

Use forms such as `portanto`, `no entanto`, `além disso`, `ora`, `pois bem` and
`em suma` only when the logical transition warrants them. A connector should
make a relationship visible, not announce that another sentence has begun.

For reflective hypotheses, consider `caso` with the subjunctive when it produces
a more composed and natural period. Do not apply this mechanically.

Read the finished text aloud. Rewrite a passage if its word order, pauses, or
emphasis feel natural only when mentally reconstructed in English.

## Place modifiers over the right idea

For every adjective, comparative, adverb, adjectival phrase, or prepositional
adjunct, identify both its grammatical head and the property or relation it is
meant to express. Agreement alone is insufficient: the modifier must also form
a natural Portuguese collocation and describe something that its head can
logically bear.

This positional and semantic review follows Almeida, §§790-791. Apply the
antecedent, voice, agreement and circumstance checks in
[the grammar and style reference](grammar-and-style.md) at the same time.

Before translating an English attributive modifier as a Portuguese adjective,
decide whether it denotes an inherent quality or a relation of purpose, scope,
audience, means, readiness, or suitability. Express relational meanings with
the conventional complement or clause—such as `de uso geral`, `para equipes`
or `pronto para produção`—instead of forcing them into a one-word adjective.

Do not make an adjective carry an omitted idea. If breadth belongs to scope,
range, or applicability, name that dimension or use a verb such as `abranger`,
`atender` or `servir`; do not describe the person or tool itself as broad.
Likewise, make the dimension of a comparison explicit whenever `mais` or
`menos` could apply to several properties.

Treat an adjunct introduced by a preposition as a concrete relation—such as
origin, possession, purpose, means, cause, domain, or destination. Keep it near
the word it modifies. When a chain of nouns and prepositions leaves that
relation vague, replace it with a clause or a more precise verb instead of
adding another modifier.

## Control cadence

Cadence comes from syntax and reasoning, not from a repeated rhetorical mold.
Let the length of a sentence follow the thought it carries. A longer period is
welcome when subordination and punctuation keep its architecture visible; a
short sentence is welcome when the thought is actually complete.

Following Almeida, §784, remove nearby repetitions of the same word or lexical
root when they do not serve precision or deliberate emphasis. Preserve
repetition that contributes force, contrast, rhythm or specification. Resolve
empty repetition by restructuring the sentence or its references, not by
cycling through ornamental synonyms.

Do not impose sentence-length quotas. Under Almeida, §885 and the discussion on
p. 487 of the 18th edition, a period succeeds through complete sense and the
placement and connection of its clauses. A long period may be lucid; several
short sentences may still be mechanical.

## Deconstruct the draft before approving it

After drafting, perform a silent syntactic and lexical deconstruction. This is
an editorial working pass, not part of the artifact returned to the user.

For each sentence:

1. Identify the subject and the real-world referent intended by it, the finite
   verb, its complements, each subordinate clause, and every coordination.
   Reduce the sentence to this clause skeleton before judging its polish.
   Resolve every possessive and relative clause to one unmistakable antecedent;
   classify each use of `se`; and identify the subject and temporal or logical
   relation of every gerund. Apply
   [the grammar and style reference](grammar-and-style.md), especially §§329,
   376, 393, 403-407 and 942-944.
2. Restore the generic Portuguese head understood behind a proper name,
   acronym, or retained foreign noun. For a proper name, use established
   article and gender when the relevant source or speech community provides
   them. A category assigned in the current sentence is not evidence of that
   convention: the predicate in `O Google é uma empresa` does not license `A
   Google`. If usage cannot be established, state the generic head explicitly
   or avoid an article instead of guessing. For common technical nouns, derive
   agreement from the Portuguese concept they denote.
3. Assign every adjective, comparative, adverb, participle, and prepositional
   adjunct to its exact grammatical head. Paraphrase the quality or relation
   that it expresses. If the paraphrase belongs to an omitted concept, to
   another member of the sentence, or vaguely to the sentence as a whole,
   reconstruct the clause.
4. Classify an adjectival form as a simple property, a resulting state, an
   ongoing process, a means, a purpose, or a recurring behavior. A past
   participle should not be retained merely because an English modifier or a
   neighboring technical register suggests it. Choose the conventional
   Portuguese adjective, complement, or finite verb that expresses the
   intended class. Ask whether the noun itself underwent the action encoded by
   the participle, as prescribed by Almeida, §938. If only the production or
   delivery mechanism is automatic, for example, write `relatórios gerados
   automaticamente`, not `relatórios automatizados`.
5. Verify that coordinated members occupy the same grammatical and conceptual
   level. A list is defective when it mixes, without an expressed relation,
   such things as an object, a process, a delivery channel, an outcome, or an
   umbrella category that subsumes another member. For coordinated predicates,
   reconstruct the subject, auxiliary and complements of each predicate
   separately. Do not share an auxiliary when the predicates require different
   subjects or agreement. Rebuild the hierarchy instead of polishing the list.
6. Test each collocation independently of the surrounding sentence. A phrase
   that becomes plausible only because the reader can infer the intended
   slogan, source expression, or omitted relation is not yet natural
   Portuguese.
7. Look at the complete clause skeleton again. If it reproduces a generic
   foreign-language template slot by slot, discard that skeleton and redraft
   from the hierarchy of propositions; changing individual words is
   insufficient.

Complete this pass on the rewritten sentence as well. Do not return a phrase
that still requires charitable interpretation to sound idiomatic.

### Worked deconstructions

These examples demonstrate the review operation; they are not templates to
copy.

**Proper-name gender is established independently of its predicate.**

Suppose the source says `recursos do Atlas` and `acesse o Atlas`. The draft `A
Atlas é uma plataforma de pesquisa` has:

- subject and referent: the product `Atlas`;
- gender evidence: the source's `do Atlas` and `o Atlas`;
- predicate nominative: `uma plataforma de pesquisa`.

The predicate classifies the product but does not rename the grammatical head
understood in the subject. Write `O Atlas é uma plataforma de pesquisa`. If
the established usage were unavailable, write `A plataforma Atlas...` or
`Atlas oferece...` rather than guessing.

**A participle must describe the noun that carries it.**

In `receba relatórios automatizados por e-mail`, ask what underwent
automation. If the reports are generated and sent by an automatic process, the
mechanism is automatic; the reports themselves were not converted from manual
to automatic. Write `receba relatórios gerados e enviados automaticamente por
e-mail`, or use another conventional construction that names the actual
relation.

**A source label does not freeze English noun order in running prose.**

Suppose a source field names a feature `Orion SDK`. The lexical items belong to
the feature name, but their relation still needs Portuguese syntax when used in
a sentence: write `o SDK do Orion`. Preserve `Orion SDK` as an indivisible
string only when reproducing an interface label, quotation, identifier, or
explicitly required display name. Merely receiving the value in a field called
`feature` does not create that requirement.

**Redraft the institutional skeleton instead of replacing its words.**

Consider `A plataforma Acme transforma tarefas em resultados. Ela reúne
quadros, alertas e integrações para que as equipes planejem, executem e
entreguem melhor.` The first sentence relies on an abstract transformation;
the second mixes interface objects, notifications, and connections before
ending in a rhythmic sequence of generic actions. A reconstruction might read:
`Na plataforma Acme, a equipe organiza o trabalho em quadros compartilhados e
recebe um aviso quando uma tarefa muda de estado. As integrações mantêm essas
alterações sincronizadas com os outros serviços usados no projeto.` The
rewrite succeeds because each feature now has its own relation and consequence,
not because it substitutes synonyms into the original slots.

## Use punctuation structurally

For punctuation, the prescriptions in
[the punctuation guide](punctuation.md), derived from Almeida, §§947-975, are
authoritative for grammatical admissibility. Editorial review may reject
overuse or an artificial AI pattern, but it must not call a construction
ungrammatical when the cited prescription admits it. Judge the function of the
mark in its sentence before applying an antipattern.
Do not replace the guide with frequency targets or a shorter list of preferred
marks.

## Remove recurrent artificial constructions

Rewrite these patterns whenever they appear as defaults rather than as
deliberate, necessary choices. When the source contains one, preserve its
propositions and logical relationship without copying its rhetorical frame.

1. **Manufactured anticipation.** Openings such as `E é aqui que...`, `É nesse
   momento que...` or `Agora vem a parte importante...` delay the point in
   order to simulate emphasis. State the point and develop its consequence.
2. **Canned antithesis.** Do not introduce frames such as `não é X, mas Y`,
   `X, não Y` and `não apenas X, mas também Y` in a new draft or stylistic
   rewrite. During correction, preserve a competent author's deliberate,
   exact contrast; rewrite only mechanical or defective uses. Always preserve
   quotations and source text that must remain exact. Do not repair the mold
   merely by replacing it with `além de` or `mais do que`; reconstruct the
   relationship between the propositions.
3. **Colon as a punch line.** A setup that withholds its point until a slogan
   after the colon is an English rhetorical mold. Express the relationship
   directly. Do not mistake a legitimate verbless enunciation or enumeration
   for this defect.
4. **Rhythmic parataxis.** Sequences of short clauses, especially artificial
   groups of three, can sound polished while leaving the relationship among
   ideas unstated. Lists of parallel labels create the same problem. Give the
   propositions distinct syntactic roles and express their relationship instead
   of translating or replacing each slot individually.
5. **Sentence fragments.** Give prose a finite verb and a syntactic center.
   Fragments remain appropriate for natural labels, buttons, headings, and
   other interface elements whose function requires them.
6. **English calques.** Reject a literal transfer when it produces unnatural
   Portuguese syntax, assigns a word a meaning the intended domain does not
   recognize, or obscures agency and the relationship among ideas. Judge the
   whole construction in context: establish the source proposition, verify how
   the intended audience names the concept, and test whether the Portuguese
   sentence stands on its own without mental reconstruction in English. Do not
   maintain a blacklist of words. When the construction fails, rewrite it
   rather than exchange one suspect word for a longer literal paraphrase.
7. **Generic metadiscourse.** Remove expressions such as `é importante
   ressaltar`, `vale destacar`, `nesse sentido` and automatic closing formulas
   when they add no relation or qualification.
8. **Influencer cadence.** Avoid emojis, forced slang, breathless one-line
   paragraphs, motivational conclusions, engagement bait, and manufactured
   intimacy unless the source explicitly requires that content.
9. **Argument by formatting.** Do not turn reasoning into bullets merely to
   make it scannable. Use lists and tables for genuinely enumerable or
   comparable material.
10. **Institutional product template.** Do not let product copy proceed
    mechanically from a category definition, to a feature inventory, to an
    abstract benefit or a sequence of audience actions. This progression often
    reproduces a foreign-language marketing template even when every sentence
    is grammatical. Determine the distinguishing proposition, give supporting
    details syntactic roles that reflect their hierarchy, and vary the
    structure according to that relationship. As a diagnostic, abstract the
    wording: `[product] is a [category] that [abstract benefit]. It brings
    together [list] so that [audience] can [verb], [verb], and [verb].` If the
    Portuguese draft maps onto those slots, redraft it from the propositions
    unless that exact hierarchy is independently warranted.
11. **Empty gathering.** In product copy, use `reunir` as the main predicate
    only when bringing previously separate things together is itself a relevant
    proposition. Do not use it merely to give a finite verb to an inventory of
    features. Give each selected feature its actual use, mechanism, or relation
    to the others.
12. **Metaphorical conversion without transformation.** Use `transformar X em
    Y` only when X actually becomes Y or when the metaphor names a precise,
    intelligible change. If X instead informs, supports, measures, causes, or
    serves as evidence for Y, state that relation with its own verb or
    complement. The fact that English marketing commonly uses `turn X into Y`
    does not establish the same collocation in Portuguese.

## Respect artifact constraints

The artifact changes the shape of the prose, not the meaning of good
Portuguese.

First identify the artifact's communicative center: what the reader should
understand, feel, or do after reading it. Treat an explicitly requested tone or
emotion as part of the artifact's meaning, not as ornament to be removed.
Express it through the selection and order of information, the force of the
verbs, and the degree of directness appropriate to the occasion. It does not
require slogans, forced intimacy, exclamation marks, or other prefabricated
signs of emotion. Respect the requested degree: a restrained tone does not
license an intensifier merely because the underlying emotion was requested.
Naming an emotion directly can be natural, but it is not a substitute for
making the artifact itself carry that tone.

Select and order only the facts that serve that center. Do not turn a hero into
procedural documentation, a channel message into a release note, an
announcement into a changelog, or a short explanation into an inventory of
implementation details. Preserve the exact name of a feature or concept when it
is more informative than a generic paraphrase.

Compression permits omission, not semantic collapse. Retain the verb or
relation that explains what each selected fact means; do not place unlike
actions, quantities, or criteria under one predicate merely to save words.
Likewise, do not invent a progression, contrast, or causal link to connect
facts that the source only enumerates.

### Product and interface copy

Optimize for immediate comprehension, consistency, and the available space.
Make buttons express actions, labels name content, help text answer the relevant
question, and error messages describe the known problem and recovery path.
Never invent a cause or recovery step. Fragments may be natural for short
controls; supporting text should remain syntactically complete. When the
surrounding interface has already established a feature's full display name,
refer to it in nearby supporting copy by its unambiguous Portuguese category
noun instead of repeating an English-order brand-plus-category label. Preserve
the full string where it functions as the visible label; use natural anaphora
in the sentence that follows.

### Titles and subtitles

Express one useful proposition within the available space. Remove subordinate
detail before compressing the remaining words into an unnatural slogan. Do not
use headline drama to compensate for weak information.

### Documentation

Describe stable behavior, usage, constraints, interfaces, and durable rationale.
Keep commands, paths, variables, states, and errors literal. Separate facts
from recommendations, and stated capabilities from consequences that merely
appear to follow from them. Do not include ticket numbers, current
implementation progress, conversation history, or future work unless the
document is explicitly a project tracker.

### Summaries and edited transcripts

Preserve attribution, chronology, decisions, open questions, uncertainty, and
commitments. Remove oral repetition only when the requested artifact is edited
prose. When the source states a comparison or conclusion that is central to the
artifact, preserve its terms instead of replacing it with a vague appraisal. A
request for a verbatim transcript falls outside this skill.

### Email and messages

Answer the main point early and preserve the relationship, degree of formality,
and requested emotional temperature. A complete artifact may use the ordinary
structural conventions its function calls for, such as a subject, greeting,
call to action, or neutral close. Do not invent a personal relationship,
gratitude, apology, availability, deadline, commitment, sender identity, or
emotion that the request does not establish. Conversely, do not flatten
explicitly requested enthusiasm, concern, warmth, or urgency merely because
similar effects are often abused in promotional writing.

### Commercial copy

Tie value to a stated mechanism, use, or consequence. Keep claims traceable to
the source. Do not invent urgency, testimonials, comparisons, guarantees, or
numbers. Add a call to action only when the artifact's function requires one.
Make the main predicate carry an informative relation: what the reader does,
what the product changes in the work, or how a stated mechanism produces a
consequence. A verb that merely holds an inventory together does not become
informative because the listed features are specific. If removing the list
leaves only a category label or an abstract benefit, reconstruct the passage
around the mechanism or use.

### Reflective and explanatory prose

Frame the question or thesis early enough to orient the reader. Let each
paragraph develop a step in the reasoning. Use examples to test or clarify the
claim, and end when the reasoning has reached its consequence rather than with
a mechanical recap.

## Perform an editorial review

Review the complete artifact after drafting:

1. Check the artifact contract before polishing sentences: purpose, audience,
   format, length, register, requested tone or emotion, informational center,
   required facts, protected meaning, and literal tokens. A fluent text that
   answers a different kind of request is a failed draft. Compare every token
   explicitly designated as a literal or identifier character for character
   with the source; do not translate, inflect or regularize it.
2. Check certainty, agency, modality, attribution, commitments, and scope
   against the supplied content.
3. Locate sentences whose order or idiom still depends on English.
4. Inspect every retained technical term in its semantic and grammatical
   context: identify the Portuguese concept it denotes, then verify gender,
   number, articles, contractions, adjectives, references, and its relation to
   nearby product or brand names. Preserve an exact display name only when the
   source requires it; otherwise, preserving its words does not require
   preserving English noun order or suppressing the Portuguese preposition
   that expresses origin, ownership, or association. Treat unresolved article
   or gender for a proper name as an unresolved sentence, not as permission to
   guess. If first-party usage cannot be inspected or found, recast with an
   explicit common-noun head or a finite clause whose agreement is established.
5. Perform the full silent deconstruction above, including clause skeleton,
   referent and gender, modifier scope, lexical class, collocation, and
   coordination hierarchy. Read each finite verb with its own clause and verify
   that every required complement is grammatically present rather than merely
   recoverable from a nearby sentence. For every adjacent generic noun and
   proper name, state their relation—such as origin, ownership or
   association—and verify that Portuguese expresses it instead of merely
   juxtaposing the nouns. Expand every coordinated predicate into a complete
   standalone clause by restoring its subject and auxiliary. If the expanded
   clauses require different agreement, make the omitted auxiliary explicit
   unless the ellipsis remains both unmistakable and idiomatic. Compression
   must not conceal a mismatch; use independent predicates or genuinely
   parallel nominal groups when necessary.
6. Inspect connectors and punctuation for structural purpose.
7. Verify that the chosen facts and their order fit the requested artifact
   rather than merely reproducing the source's hierarchy.
8. Remove only artificial uses of the constructions listed above; do not
   suppress a legitimate rhetorical choice or an emotion required by the
   artifact.
9. Read for cadence and accidental lexical repetition across sentences and
   paragraphs, not just within each sentence.
10. Rewrite constructions rather than exchanging isolated words.
11. Recheck the complete artifact against step 1. The review is not successful
    if syntactic cleanup has made the text colder, less direct, less personal,
    or otherwise less fit for its stated purpose.
