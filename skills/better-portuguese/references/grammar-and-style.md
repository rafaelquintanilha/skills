# Grammar and style

This reference records the non-punctuation prescriptions from Napoleão Mendes
de Almeida that inform Better Portuguese. It is a review framework, not a
license to reproduce every preference of a conservative twentieth-century
grammar in contemporary PT-BR.

## Bibliographic reference

ALMEIDA, Napoleão Mendes de. *Gramática metódica da língua portuguesa*. 18th
ed. São Paulo: Saraiva, 1965. 579 p.

The public research copy is the [Google Books record and searchable
preview](https://books.google.com/books?id=FPBPAAAAMAAJ), digitized from the
University of Texas copy. Paragraph numbers are the canonical locators because
pagination changes between editions. The page numbers below belong to the 18th
edition.

The operative guidance is paraphrased in English. The diagnostic examples are
new and test the same grammatical phenomena with unrelated material; they are
not quotations or templates.

## Resolve references before polishing

### Possessives

Source: §329, p. 167.

`Seu`, `sua`, `seus` and `suas` become ambiguous when more than one
third-person referent can possess the following noun. Place the possessed
object near an unmistakable possessor or recast the relation. Do not rely on the
reader to choose the intended antecedent.

Diagnostic:

> `Marina apresentou Laura à sua gerente.`

The sentence must be recast if the context does not establish whose manager is
meant.

### Relative clauses

Source: §376, pp. 189-190.

A relative clause must attach unmistakably to its antecedent. Inspect
constructions of the form `N de N que...` and any sequence with two plausible
antecedents. Moving the clause is not enough if the new position remains
ambiguous; name the relation.

Diagnostic:

> `Revimos a configuração do serviço que falhou.`

Without context, `que falhou` may describe either the configuration or the
service.

## Identify voice, agency and circumstance

### Uses of `se`

Source: §393, pp. 197-198; §§403-407, pp. 202-206.

When a plural subject occurs with `se`, determine whether the construction is
reflexive, reciprocal, pronominal or passive. If context does not settle the
relation, express it directly. In a passive construction with `se`, the verb
agrees with the passive subject in formal prose.

Diagnostic:

> `Os serviços se atualizam.`

This may mean that each service updates itself, that they update one another,
or merely that someone updates them. The surrounding text must make the
intended mechanism recoverable.

### Gerunds

Source: §§942-944, pp. 506-509.

A gerund adds a circumstance to a governing action. Identify its subject and
its temporal or logical relation to that action. Rewrite a dangling gerund or a
chain of successive `-ndo` forms when the connection is unclear. Do not reject
a gerund whose subject, timing and function are precise.

Diagnostic:

> `O agente abriu o arquivo, identificando a falha.`

The sentence should make clear whether the identification happened while the
file was opened, resulted from opening it, or describes a later action.

## Let form follow the intended relation

### Position and scope

Source: §§790-791, pp. 438-444.

The position of a word or phrase can determine what it modifies and what the
sentence emphasizes. Direct order is ordinary, not mandatory. Accept inversion
when it serves a clear relation or expressive purpose; reject it when a
modifier acquires the wrong head or ambiguous scope.

### Participles

Source: §938, pp. 504-505.

A participle normally denotes an undergone action or its resulting state,
although Portuguese has lexicalized exceptions. During review, ask whether the
noun actually underwent the process named by the participle. If the intended
idea is an inherent property, a manner of operation, a means, a purpose or a
recurring behavior, use the conventional adjective, complement or finite verb
instead.

### Partitive collectives

Source: §711, p. 400.

With a partitive collective, agreement can follow the singular collective or
the represented individuals when the predicate applies to them separately. If
the action belongs to the collective as one unit, prefer the singular. Decide
from meaning rather than applying a mechanical number rule.

## Preserve purposeful style

### Repetition

Source: §784, pp. 434-435.

Repetition is justified when it contributes emphasis, contrast, rhythm or
semantic specification. When it adds nothing, it becomes a defect. Review it
with caution and restraint: do not replace purposeful repetition with
ornamental synonyms merely to vary vocabulary.

### Periods and clause architecture

Source: §885, p. 474; discussion on p. 487.

A period contains one or more clauses that form a complete sense. Its quality
depends on the placement and connection of those clauses, not on a maximum
length or sentence count. Prefer a coherent compound period to either an
overloaded sentence with hidden relations or a mechanical sequence of short
sentences.

### Correctness and individual style

Source: §§980-981, p. 531.

Grammar governs the correct combination of words used to express thought;
style remains the writer's individual manner of expression. A syntactic review
must therefore improve correctness and clarity without normalizing every
artifact into the same cadence, register or rhetorical temperature.

## Deliberate exclusions

Do not import narrow prescriptions merely because Almeida defended them. This
standard does not mechanically maximize enclisis or mesoclisis, adopt disputed
preferences such as `aumentar de 20%`, or treat his polemics about linguistics
as writing rules. Contemporary Brazilian usage, the artifact's function and the
writer's intentional voice remain material after grammatical correctness has
been secured.
